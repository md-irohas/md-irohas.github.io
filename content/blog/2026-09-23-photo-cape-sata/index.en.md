## Story

In March 2026, I visited Cape Sata in Kagoshima Prefecture during a trip through Miyazaki and Kagoshima.

I left my hotel in Kagoshima City in the morning and drove around Aira Caldera to Sakurajima.
After driving a full circuit around Sakurajima, I headed south along the Osumi Peninsula to explore Cape Sata before returning to Kagoshima City.

### Sakurajima

[Sakurajima (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakurajima) is an active volcano in Kagoshima Bay, also known as Kinko Bay.
It was once an island, but lava from the great eruption of 1914 connected it to the Osumi Peninsula.

Instead of taking the ferry from Kagoshima City, I followed the road around the bay to Sakurajima.

Just before reaching Sakurajima, I stopped at Tarumizu roadside rest area.
There is a footbath here, but it was still empty that early in the morning, and the mountain was hidden in the clouds.

I crossed Ushine Bridge to reach Sakurajima.

The road around Sakurajima is about 36 km long -- longer than I had expected.
I drove counterclockwise, passing shelters built for use during volcanic eruptions.

I stopped near Sakurajima Port along the way, but the weather had yet to improve.
From the parking lot, the summit was still hidden in the clouds.

### Cape Sata

Cape Sata, in Minamiosumi, Kagoshima Prefecture, is the southernmost point of mainland Japan.

After about two hours of driving south from Sakurajima along the Osumi Peninsula, I finally reached the cape.

I parked beside the tourist information center.
A large [gajumaru (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_microcarpa), a type of banyan tree, stood next to the parking lot.

Cape Sata Lighthouse, visible in the photos, stands on an offshore island and cannot be reached by the walking trail.
My destination was Cape Sata Observation Deck, visible on the right side of the panorama.

The observation deck is about an 800-meter walk from the tourist information center.
I followed the red route on the trail map on the way out and returned along the yellow route via the former lighthouse keepers’ quarters.
