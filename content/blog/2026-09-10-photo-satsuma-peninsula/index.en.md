## Story

In March 2026, I spent a day driving around the Satsuma Peninsula in Kagoshima Prefecture.

I left my hotel in Kagoshima City in the morning and visited Chiran Peace Museum.
I then walked around Cape Nagasakibana, the southernmost point of the Satsuma Peninsula, before relaxing at Tamatebako Onsen and returning to Kagoshima City.


### Chiran Peace Museum

My first stop was Chiran Peace Museum in Minamikyushu.

During the final months of World War II, Chiran served as a departure base for [Imperial Japanese Army special attack units (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Special_Attack_Units), commonly known outside Japan as kamikaze.
The museum displays portrait photographs, farewell letters, personal effects, historical materials, and military aircraft related to the pilots.

- Chiran Peace Museum: https://www.chiran-tokkou.jp/en/

Photography is prohibited in most parts of the museum.

Through videos and original artifacts, the exhibits trace the lives of the pilots and the circumstances that led to their final sorties.
Given the weight of the subject, I will not go into detail here, but the exhibits left me with a great deal to reflect on.

I spent about two hours at the museum.
Anyone wishing to examine every artifact and video carefully may want to allow even more time.

Museum Chiran, located next door, was closed that day, so I was unable to visit it.

After leaving the museum, I stopped by the nearby Chiran Tokko Peace Kannon Hall.
It was built on the former site of Chiran Airfield in 1955 to commemorate the pilots and pray for peace.

### Cape Nagasakibana

After leaving Chiran, I headed to Cape Nagasakibana in Ibusuki, the southernmost point of the Satsuma Peninsula.

- Cape Nagasakibana (Japanese only): https://www.ibusuki.or.jp/tourism/view/nagasakibana/

{{< google-maps-2
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/embed?pb=!1m14!1m8!1m3!1d218388.96006030668!2d130.5563697!3d31.2116548!3m2!1i1024!2i768!4f13.1!3m3!1m2!1s0x353dd3a220dd5899%3A0x1d2be324daf0b540!2z6ZW35bSO6by7!5e0!3m2!1sen!2sjp!4v1789267711581!5m2!1sen!2sjp"

There are several souvenir shops and tourist facilities with parking lots around the cape, which made it difficult to tell where I should park.
I eventually left the car at a free municipal parking lot beside the road and walked toward the cape.

After a few minutes, the cape and its lighthouse came into view.

### Tamatebako Onsen

After walking around Cape Nagasakibana, I stopped at Healthy Land's Tamatebako Onsen on my way back to Kagoshima City.

The name tamatebako refers to the mysterious box that Urashima Taro receives in the folktale associated with the area.

- Healthy Land Open-Air Bath "Tamatebako Onsen" (Japanese only): https://www.ibusuki.or.jp/spa/open/tamatebako/

{{< google-maps-2
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/embed?pb=!1m14!1m8!1m3!1d218190.74266147014!2d130.6943217!3d31.2973778!3m2!1i1024!2i768!4f13.1!3m3!1m2!1s0x353dd3053d3898cd%3A0xc54e0013f3b14aa4!2z44OY44Or44K344O844Op44Oz44OJ6Zyy5aSp6aKo5ZGCIOOBn-OBvuOBpueusea4qeaziQ!5e0!3m2!1sen!2sjp!4v1789267779646!5m2!1sen!2sjp"
  >}}

{{< figure
    src="IMG_0512.jpg"

    caption="Tamatebako Onsen"

    >}}

The steep-sided mountain visible from the parking lot was also striking.

{{< figure
    src="IMG_0511.jpg"
    alt="IMG_0511.jpg"
    caption="Mountain viewed from the parking lot"
    class="w50"
    >}}

The bathing area consists entirely of open-air baths, with an unobstructed and expansive view of the sea.

There were several baths at different temperatures.
Even the coolest one was quite hot, and despite the chilly March air, I could not stay in it for very long.

I later learned from the official website that the spring emerges at 100.4°C.
The water is cooled by adding water and then flows continuously through the baths.

After taking my time at the onsen, I returned to my hotel in Kagoshima City and brought the day's travels to an end.
